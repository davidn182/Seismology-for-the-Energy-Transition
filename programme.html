About the meeting

This three-day meeting at TU Delft trains seismologists to monitor geothermal and geological storage operations, including CO₂ storage and underground hydrogen storage (UHS). Day one covers earthquake catalogs. Using the open induced-seismicity Groningen catalog, participants estimate the magnitude of completeness, track the b-value over space and time, and test the criteria that separate induced from natural earthquakes. Day two covers seismic imaging. Participants build seismic-velocity models and get hands-on training in active-source data acquisition and processing. Day three covers seismic-network design, a method most seismology courses do not include. Participants first learn the principles behind detecting and locating microseismicity, then explore optimal methods for placing sensors.

Each day combines a keynote talk, contributed talks and a hands-on workshop using open data. Participants also visit real infrastructure: DAPwell, TU Delft’s geothermal doublet on campus, built for research. The meeting also provides an icebreaker and opportunities for collaboration. Participants bring a laptop and receive the datasets and set-up instructions two weeks before the meeting.

Who should come

Seismologists at early and later career stages, and professionals at national agencies and companies working on the monitoring of induced seismicity in geothermal energy, geological storage, including CCS and UHS, and related subsurface energy operations.

We specifically encourage researchers from countries where seismic monitoring programmes for geological storage and geothermal energy are still developing. A limited number of travel grants are available.
