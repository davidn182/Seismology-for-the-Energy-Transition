Workshop on seismic monitoring tools for geothermal energy and geological storage

A three-day meeting that teaches state-of-the-art methods and brings together seismologists working on geothermal energy and geological storage, including CO₂ storage and underground hydrogen storage (UHS), with the objective of advancing the science of microseismic monitoring. Attendance is free. Participants who submit an abstract get priority.

Day 1 · Wed 12 May

Earthquake catalogs & induced seismicity

Earthquake-catalog construction and statistics, with Groningen as the reference case: two decades of extraction-induced seismicity and an open KNMI catalog. Estimate magnitude of completeness, track b-value in space and time, and apply the rate and spatial criteria used to attribute events to operations rather than tectonics.

Seismic imaging & velocity models

Accurate hypocentres and focal mechanisms depend on velocity models. Velocity-model construction and subsurface imaging from active-source data, including S-wave velocities extracted from vibroseis recordings, and passive-source seismic interferometry. Site visit to DAPwell.

Day 3 · Fri 14 May

Passive monitoring

Sensor placement determines which microearthquakes are detected and how well they are located, yet network geometry is often overlooked. Principles of detection and location, signal-to-noise thresholds, azimuthal coverage and depth resolution, then optimal network design for a Dutch CCS or geothermal field.

Why this workshop

Across Europe, geothermal doublets and geological storage sites, including for CO₂ and UHS, pose seismologists the same problem despite being commissioned at pace and for different purposes. They involve the injection and, in some cases, withdrawal of fluids from a reservoir, and can perturb stress on pre-existing faults through pressure and temperature changes. Often the only early-warning signal is microseismicity.

What microseismicity means for these operations differs. Microseismicity migrating away from an injection well may trace a cooling reservoir, fluid migration beyond the intended storage complex, such as leakage, or a fault that was already close to failure.

Understanding its origin is a measurement problem: detecting events in noisy seismic records, estimating their locations precisely enough to attribute them to a process, and separating operational from background seismicity. This requires three ingredients, one per day of the workshop: (i) knowledge of the baseline seismicity of the field, (ii) an accurate P- and S-wave velocity model for locating events, and (iii) seismic-network coverage adequate for detecting and locating the microseismicity.

Who should come

Seismologists at early and later career stages, and professionals at national agencies and companies working on the monitoring of induced seismicity in geothermal energy, geological storage, including CCS and UHS, and related subsurface energy operations.

We specifically encourage researchers from countries where seismic monitoring programmes for geological storage and geothermal energy are still developing. A limited number of travel grants are available.
