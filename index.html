Scientific motivation

Europe is working towards climate neutrality by 2050, in line with the Paris Agreement. The subsurface is central to reaching that goal. Geoenergy uses it as a source of heat and as a storage for carbon and hydrogen. Cities and industries are replacing natural-gas heating with geothermal heat. TU Delft heats its own campus from a geothermal doublet. The Porthos project will store CO₂ captured in the Port of Rotterdam in depleted gas fields beneath the North Sea. Salt caverns will store hydrogen, as Gasunie is developing at Zuidwending, to balance supply from wind and solar.

Each of these projects needs seismologists. Before drilling, we map the historical seismicity of a site. That seismicity reveals the stresses, faults and structures that make a productive geothermal reservoir or a secure CO₂ storage. Seismic methods image the subsurface without drilling into every area of interest. Once operations start, seismologists monitor how stresses evolve. Microseismicity shows where fluids migrate and confirms whether they stay within the intended reservoir. Monitoring also anticipates induced seismicity before it is felt. Done well, it safeguards delivery of clean energy.

This workshop builds those skills. It covers the three ingredients of monitoring: a baseline catalog, an accurate P- and S-wave velocity model, and a network designed to detect and locate the events that matter.

Day 1 · Wed 12 May

Earthquake catalogs & induced seismicity

Earthquake-catalog construction and statistics, with Groningen as the reference case: two decades of extraction-induced seismicity and an open KNMI catalog. Estimate magnitude of completeness, track b-value in space and time, and apply the rate and spatial criteria used to attribute events to operations rather than tectonics.

Day 2 · Thu 13 May

Seismic imaging & velocity models

Accurate hypocentres and focal mechanisms depend on velocity models. Velocity-model construction and subsurface imaging from active-source data, including S-wave velocities extracted from vibroseis recordings, and passive-source seismic interferometry. Site visit to DAPwell.

Day 2 programme →
Day 3 · Fri 14 May

Passive monitoring

Sensor placement determines which microearthquakes are detected and how well they are located, yet network geometry is often overlooked. Principles of detection and location, signal-to-noise thresholds, azimuthal coverage and depth resolution, then optimal network design for a Dutch CCS or geothermal field.
